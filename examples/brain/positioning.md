# Positioning — bike-shop-scheduling-v1 (worked example)

## The wedge (one moment / one job)

The Saturday morning during spring tune-up season when the repair queue is three bikes deep,
the paper ticket board is illegible, and a customer is standing at the counter asking where
their bike is. That's the moment the manual system visibly fails — not "scheduling software"
in the abstract.

## The pain, in their words

> ⏳ Seed language, not a real quote — mark as a guess until replaced by an actual reply:
> "I had bikes stacked three deep and no idea whose was next."

## Message angle (the fork we chose)

- **We lead with:** the specific chaos moment — the three-deep queue, the illegible board, the
  customer waiting at the counter.
- **We deliberately do NOT lead with:** "modernize your shop with software" or any generic
  small-business-SaaS framing. A shop owner doesn't think of themselves as needing "software" —
  they think of themselves as needing to stop losing tickets. Leading with the product category
  instead of the moment reads as a pitch instead of recognition.

## Primary value proposition (the anchor)

SpokeDesk keeps every ticket matched to the right bike and the right customer automatically,
so a three-deep Saturday stays manageable instead of a scramble.

## Proof / differentiation

- **Why us, why now:** built originally as an internal tool for a single shop's own backlog
  problem (fictional origin story, used for tone only — do not present as a verified claim in
  a real fill unless it's true).
- **The defensible claim:** it imports an existing paper or spreadsheet system in one step, so
  switching costs almost nothing to try.

## Guardrails (do-not-say)

Never imply the shop's current paper system is unprofessional or behind the times — it's
worked well enough to build a business on. Never claim the tool replaces a mechanic's
judgment about the repair itself, only the tracking around it. No fake urgency ("only 3 spots
left this month") — a shop owner will recognize manufactured scarcity in an unsolicited email
immediately and it costs more trust than it buys.
