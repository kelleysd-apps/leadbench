# Vertical Profile — bike-shop-scheduling-v1 (worked example)

## Who they are

- **Who exactly are we targeting?** The owner-operator of an independent bicycle repair shop
  — usually also the shop's head mechanic. Not a purchasing manager, not a retail buyer at a
  chain: the person who owns the business and personally feels a double-booked Saturday.
- **Org type / size:** Independently owned single-location repair shops, 1–6 mechanics on
  staff including the owner. Not a chain, not a big-box sporting-goods service counter.
- **Where do they sit in the org?** Owner-operator or, in slightly larger shops, a service
  manager the owner has delegated scheduling to. Either way, the person who feels the pain
  directly is also the person who can say yes.

## Where they gather (cold-reachable surfaces)

- **Primary channel:** shop websites and public shop-listing directories — nearly every
  independent bike shop publishes a public contact email for repair bookings, which is exactly
  the surface where the pain (manual scheduling) is visible.
- **Communities / associations:** the Independent Cycle Retailers Guild (fictional trade
  association, example.org) and local shop-owner Facebook groups.
- **Signals that mark someone as warm before you ever reach out:** public reviews mentioning
  wait times, lost tickets, or "call ahead" booking; shop social posts about being "slammed" or
  "backed up" during tune-up season; a shop still advertising "walk-ins only, no online
  booking."
- **Events / publications:** regional cycling trade shows (fictional, unspecified) — not yet
  used as a sourcing channel for this focus.

## How they buy

- **Motion:** self-serve. The owner tries the product themselves; there is no procurement
  process.
- **Budget reality:** discretionary — a $19–$79/month tool is a decision one person makes
  without approval, usually the owner's personal call.
- **Sales cycle:** short — days, not weeks. A shop owner who replies during a backlog will
  often start a trial the same day.
- **Who else is in the decision:** almost always the owner alone. In multi-mechanic shops, a
  service manager may influence but rarely blocks.

## What makes our product a must-have for them

- **The acute, frequent pain:** during peak season, the shop's paper ticket board or booking
  spreadsheet stops matching reality — tickets get lost, two customers get told the same slot,
  and nobody can tell at a glance whose bike is next.
- **The "I'd pay for that right now" trigger:** the specific Saturday morning when the queue is
  three bikes deep, the paper board is illegible, and a customer is standing at the counter
  asking where their bike is.

## Disqualifiers (who looks like a fit but isn't)

Chain and big-box service counters (Rolling Wrench Bicycle Co.'s corporate-owned competitors,
for example) already run enterprise scheduling software and aren't reachable as a single
owner-decision. Retail-only shops with no repair bench have nothing for this product to
schedule. Shops that already advertise an online booking widget on their website are already
solved for — reaching them wastes a touch that could go to a shop still doing it by hand.
